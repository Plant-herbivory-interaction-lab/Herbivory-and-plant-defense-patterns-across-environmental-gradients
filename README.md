# Repository details: Herbivory and plant defense patterns across a latitudenal gradient.

## *Project overview*
Plants experience varying biotic and abiotic pressures across their geographic range. Variation in the intensity of herbivory and environmental productivity can shape plant defense traits among plant populations. Often higher herbivore numbers result in greater herbivore pressure and stronger plant defenses across species in more productive regions. While these trends are clear across species, these results are often mixed across intraspecific populations, and fewer studies quantify herbivore pressure and plant defenses across the geographic range of a plant species where underlying primary production varies substantially. We quantified defense traits and herbivory of Solanum carolinense L. among 33 populations across its entire north-south range (29–44° N), using both field observations (~14 plants per population) and a common garden experiment (~4 plants per population). We examined the effects of latitude on 1) plant defense traits (specific leaf area, trichome density and glycoalkaloid concentration), 2) herbivory, and 3) the correlation among traits and herbivory in field and common garden plants. Trichome density was higher at the center of the plant species range, and glycoalkaloid production had a negative linear trend with productivity both in the field and in the common garden. Plants in the field with higher glycoalkaloid production experienced reduced herbivory, but there was no association with plant defense traits and herbivory in the common garden. Overall, these findings suggest that different defensive traits within a single species may follow different ecological and evolutionary trends and highlight the need for trait-specific considerations when applying plant defense hypotheses at the intraspecific level.

## *Folder structure*
This repository contains the data and code used to provide results of the manuscript. The [Data folder](Data) contains the database used to store the data, the [R_code](R_code) folder contains the the code used to analyze the data, and the [Figures](Figures) folder includes the output figures for the manuscript. Each of these folders include a **README.md**  file (if appropriate) that provides details of each of the files and the data structure.
